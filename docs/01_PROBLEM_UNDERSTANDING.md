# Step 1: Understand the Problem — Who, What & Why?

> **SIH 2026 Problem Statement**: PS 26004 (S.No 4)  
> **Nodal Ministry**: Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (MDoNER)  
> **Title**: AI-Assisted Early Detection System for Osteoarthritis (OA) Risk Markers in North Eastern Region (NER)  

---

## 1. Who is Affected? (Target Beneficiaries)

- **Rural & Indigenous Communities in NER**: Farming, tea-garden laborers, and hilly terrain residents in Assam, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Mizoram, Manipur, Tripura, Arunachal Pradesh, and Sikkim.
- **Aging Rural Population**: Elderly individuals (>50 years) performing continuous joint-heavy manual labor.
- **Women in Agricultural & Domestic Labor**: High prevalence due to biomechanical stressors, squatting, carrying headloads, and post-menopausal joint wear.
- **ASHA Workers & Primary Healthcare Providers**: Frontline health workers needing simple, non-invasive digital screening tools that do not require specialized medical expertise.

---

## 2. What is the Problem?

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a degenerative joint disease where joint cartilage wears down over time, causing bones to rub together. This results in chronic pain, joint stiffness, joint space narrowing, and eventual loss of mobility.

### Key Epidemiological Evidence
1. **Jorhat (Assam) Tea-Garden Study (n=320)**:
   - **29.4%** knee OA prevalence in tea-garden workers.
   - Significantly higher in females and individuals aged >50.
2. **India Elderly Meta-Analysis (14 studies, n=5,029)**:
   - **47%** pooled prevalence among adults aged 60+.
   - Women affected at **50.7%** vs **26.2%** in men.
3. **Rural vs. Urban Comparison**:
   - Rural prevalence (**44.5%**) is virtually equal to urban (**49.3%**), but rural healthcare access is **>80% lower**.

---

## 3. Why is it Urgent & Critical in North Eastern Region (NER)?

- **Geography & Terrain**: Hilly, mountainous, and remote terrain increases physical loading on knee and hip joints while isolating communities from district hospitals.
- **Late Presentation**: Patients usually seek care only when cartilage is completely eroded (KL Grade 3/4), at which point non-surgical interventions are ineffective and joint replacement surgery (₹1.5–2.5 Lakh) is out of reach.
- **Severe Shortage of Orthopedicians & Radiologists**: Primary Health Centres (PHCs) and Health & Wellness Centres (HWCs) lack X-ray machines and radiologists.
- **Irreversibility**: Cartilage loss cannot be reversed, but early intervention (exercise therapy, activity modification, weight management) can slow progression by years if detected early.
